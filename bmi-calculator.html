What is BMI?

How is BMI calculated?

Metric: weight (kg) ÷ height (m)². Imperial: weight (lbs) × 703 ÷ height (in)².

Understanding BMI categories

Below 18.5 is underweight, 18.5–24.9 normal weight, 25–29.9 overweight, and 30 or above obesity (adults).

Understanding Body Mass Index (BMI): A Complete Guide to Measuring Your Body Weight

What Is Body Mass Index?

Body Mass Index, usually shortened to BMI, is a number calculated from a person's height and weight. It gives a quick idea of whether your weight is low, typical or high for your height. Doctors, dietitians and public health organisations use it because it is simple, free and needs no special equipment.

How Does a BMI Calculator Work?

A BMI Calculator applies the BMI formula to the measurements you enter. In metric units, your weight in kilograms is divided by your height in metres squared. In imperial units, your weight in pounds is multiplied by 703 and divided by your height in inches squared. The result is your BMI score, which is then matched to a category.

Understanding Different BMI Categories

For adults aged 20 and over, a BMI below 18.5 falls in the underweight range. A score from 18.5 to 24.9 is considered normal weight, 25.0 to 29.9 is overweight, and 30.0 or higher is classed as obesity. These BMI categories are the same for men and women, but they are not designed for children and teenagers, who need age- and sex-specific growth charts.

Why Is BMI Important for Health?

Research links very low or very high BMI values with a greater chance of health problems, including heart disease, type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure and nutritional deficiencies. Checking your BMI occasionally can be a useful early signal that it may be time to review your habits or speak to a professional.

Limitations of BMI

BMI is a screening tool, not a diagnosis. It does not measure body fat directly, so a muscular athlete may be labelled overweight while someone with little muscle may appear to be in the normal range. It also ignores where fat is stored, and it can be less accurate for older adults, pregnant women and people from different ethnic backgrounds.

How to Maintain a Healthy Weight

A healthy weight comes from steady habits rather than quick fixes. Build meals around vegetables, fruit, whole grains and lean protein. Aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate activity each week, sleep seven to nine hours, and manage stress. If your result concerns you, use it as a starting point for a conversation with a doctor.
